The site that taught me HTML was not a squadron page from 2005 — that was a placeholder story. The real one is VirtualFly.net, my first website, built in 1999 in MS FrontPage after too many hours in Jane's IAF.

## How it got made

In a WYSIWYG editor, dragging things around until it looked right, then hitting upload. The learning started when it broke — something would look wrong in the browser and fine in the editor, and the only way through was to open the HTML source and read it. `<font color="green">`. Well, obviously.

Later I rebuilt the whole thing in Dreamweaver 3 with graphics made in Fireworks, which is where I actually learned what I was doing: tables for layout, sliced images, the whole late-90s toolkit. By the PHPNuke portal era I was writing HTML by hand without thinking about it.

That is still the best on-ramp I know of: a thing you care about, visibly wrong, with a button that shows you the underneath.

## Why it explains this site

Fighter jet cockpit displays have been an interest ever since — Jane's IAF, then Falcon 4.0 — and I never really shook the aesthetic. Green symbology on black, everything boxed and labelled, no decoration that is not carrying information.

It turns out to be a perfectly reasonable way to list blog posts. An MFD is a dense, information-first interface designed to be read quickly under load. A blog index has the same job and much lower stakes.

## What happened to it

The virtualfly.net domain is long lost. What survives is in the Wayback Machine — 76 captures of the 163.com static version, the PHPNuke portal from 2003 — and a screenshot on my Behance. The recovered pages are on the workshop page now.
