LOG 2026.08.24 · 6 MIN · TIDE-CLOCK

A tide clock nobody asked for

I live nowhere near the ocean, which makes this the least useful thing I have built in a while. It started because I wanted to see whether a Cloudflare Worker could hold a tiny bit of state without me paying for anything, and it ended with a dial that turns very slowly on a page I check maybe once a week.

The maths is public domain and mostly a sum of sines. The hard part, as always, was the part I did not plan for: time zones, and the fact that tide stations report in local standard time whether or not the country observes daylight saving.

Every project I finish quickly turns out to be a project I misunderstood.

If you want the source it is on the workshop page, along with a note about the one station whose data I still cannot parse.

WORKSHOP ▸ ONCOLOGY INTERPRETATION

Oncology Interpretation

A reading surface for tumour sequencing results. It takes a variant call file, matches it against curated evidence, and lays the whole thing out so a clinician can get to the two or three variants that change a decision.

Most of the work is not the matching. It is the table — sorting, filtering, and knowing what to hide by default.

ABOUT

Hi. I build diagnostics software — the kind that takes sequencing output and turns it into something a person can act on. Before that I built a lot of websites nobody asked for, and I still do that on weekends.

I started in 2005 on a flight simulation fan site, which explains the look of this one. Fighter jet cockpit displays have been an interest ever since, and it turns out a green-on-black instrument panel is a perfectly reasonable way to list blog posts.

This site is where I keep notes so I stop losing them. If something here is useful to you, that is a happy accident.
